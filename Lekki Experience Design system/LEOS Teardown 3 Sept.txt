The verdict
The thinking in this codebase is unusually good. The continuity modules, the outbox write path, the PayFast signature encoding, the equal-share arithmetic, the permission language that says "they can see / act / finish" instead of showing a checkbox matrix — that is real product craft, and it is rare.

Two days on, the money path has been genuinely rebuilt. Money is Decimal(12,2) across all five columns with a dedicated money.ts; the epsilon fudge is gone. The client no longer sets prices — transaction.controller.ts takes a catalogue id and the server re-prices. Payments can no longer self-settle. Session resume is bound to a participantSecret instead of a first name. Allergy notes now reach the kitchen. Leave is participant-scoped. Migrations exist. Guard coverage went from 4 controllers to 12 of 16.

The risk has moved rather than gone. The arithmetic half of that rebuild is covered — money.test.ts and settlement.test.ts assert exact-cent settlement and multi-payment sums. (An earlier version of this page said there were zero tests; that was an error, from searching .spec.ts when the convention here is .test.ts.) What remains unasserted is everything above the arithmetic: server-side re-pricing, participant-secret rejection, tip base, concurrency and refunds. Every status marked closed below means the fixing construct is present, which is what reading the source can prove. For the money primitives, tests now prove more than that. For the layers above them, they do not yet.

What is untouched is the operational layer: no rate limiting, no helmet, CORS still reflects any origin, both dev secrets still fall back to their hardcoded values, and the outbox publisher still stops the whole event stream on one bad message. Plus the catalogue write API now exists and no Studio screen calls it — the endpoint is built and unreachable.

The seven that would stop me shipping
Ordered by how fast they cost you money or trust — now stamped with where each one stands after the 3 September re-read. Four of the seven are closed.

1
CLOSEDA guest can settle their own bill for free

payment.controller.ts:24 — POST /payments/:id/complete has no guard, and the guest's own browser calls it (guest.page.ts:2186). For the manual connector — which is the .env default — the browser declares its own payment successful and the transaction is marked settled. No card, no gateway, no money.

2
CLOSEDThe client sets the price

transaction.controller.ts:8 — POST /transactions accepts lines[].unitPrice from the request body and never consults the catalogue. Post the same order with unitPrice: 0.01 and the kitchen cooks the ribeye while the bill reads two cents.

3
PARTIALStudio has no authentication

studio-auth.service.ts:38 — signIn() takes an email, no password, and writes {signedIn:true} to localStorage. Now: a studioAuthGuard is applied at app.routes.ts:95, so the route is protected — but the service still has no password check, so the guard is guarding a client-side boolean. There are zero route guards in the entire app. Every Studio endpoint is unauthenticated server-side, and GET /identity/staff with no org param returns every staff member of every organisation, with emails and permissions.

4
CLOSEDOne guest tapping "Leave" ends the whole table

guest.page.ts:2240 — leave() closes the entire ExperienceSession with no participant scoping and no settlement check. "Leave" is a full-weight tab in the bottom bar. Alice pays her share and taps it; Bob, Carol and Dave lose their session mid-meal with R900 outstanding.

5
STILL OPENEvery new venue ships the same demo QR token

experience-registry.ts:87 — defaults.token: 'qr-demo-restaurant', copied verbatim into every experience of that type. Two unrelated restaurants both print a QR encoding /e/qr-demo-restaurant; their guests land in the same session and their orders route to the same kitchen. Still present: six occurrences of qr-demo in that file.

6
CLOSEDMoney is a float, and the code already knows it

schema.prisma:158,172,207,209,274 — all five monetary columns are Float (Postgres double precision). Settlement compares with a deliberate fudge: paidTowardTx + 0.001 >= total (leos.service.ts:717). That epsilon is a tenth-of-a-cent giveaway in one direction and a permanently unsettleable bill in the other.

7
PARTIALThe marketing site is invisible to AI search and has no metadata at all

index.html — title is the literal string "Lekki". No description, no canonical, no Open Graph, no structured data, no robots.txt, no sitemap.xml, no SSR or prerender. GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot do not execute JavaScript, so they see an empty <lekki-root>. Now: description, Open Graph, canonical, robots.txt and sitemap.xml all exist — real gains for Google. But angular.json still has no SSR or prerender, so GPTBot and ClaudeBot continue to fetch an empty shell. Half the fix is doing nothing yet.

Growth

SEO strategy
The honest starting position: you are not ranking badly, you are not indexable. Fixing that is mechanical. What follows is the strategy I'd run once it is.

Where you actually stand
Your marketing page is a lazy-loaded Angular route rendered entirely client-side. Googlebot runs headless Chrome and will eventually see it — on a second-wave crawl, with a delay. GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, OAI-SearchBot and Bingbot do not execute JavaScript at all. An analysis of over 500 million GPTBot fetches found zero evidence of JS execution. They fetch index.html, see <lekki-root></lekki-root>, and leave.

That matters more each quarter: AI bots are now ~4.2% of all HTML page requests and GPTBot traffic is growing 305% year over year. The failure mode is specific and worth naming — split visibility: you can rank acceptably on Google while being completely absent from the answer that ChatGPT or Claude gives a restaurateur asking "what should I use for QR ordering in South Africa". For a category that buyers research conversationally, that is the whole game.

On top of that: no robots.txt, no sitemap.xml, no meta description, no canonical, no Open Graph or Twitter card, no structured data, and no Title/Meta service usage anywhere in the app. Your Privacy and Terms links are inert <span>s — which is both an SEO signal and, for a payments product, a trust problem.

The strategy, in one paragraph
Do not chase "restaurant software" — you will lose to incumbents with a decade of domain authority. Win evaluation-stage queries instead: the buyer has already decided they want QR ordering and is now comparing vendors. Those queries are low-volume, high-intent, and largely unclaimed in the South African market. Build three page types that incumbents neglect — comparison pages ("Lekki vs X", "X alternatives"), an indexed pricing page, and use-case pages per pack (cafés, hotels, festivals, airports) — then make all of them server-rendered and schema-marked so both Google and the answer engines can read them. Comparison pages intercept buyers at the highest-intent touchpoint in a B2B journey that averages 62 touches before close.

One strategic note worth weighing: the site currently sells to operators, but every guest who scans a QR lands on your domain. That is a large, recurring, geo-tagged audience arriving on a page with no metadata. Whether the guest surface should be indexable at all is a real decision — I'd argue no for session URLs, but a public, indexed, server-rendered menu page per venue would be a genuinely strong local-SEO asset for your customers, and a reason for them to stay.

Second opinion

The Live Experience brief, scored against the code
A design brief for the split-screen Studio editor was researched separately and circulated. It is a competent document written without the codebase open — its interaction principles are sound and its engineering section describes a system that does not exist. Below, each substantive claim against what the code actually does.

WRONG
"We never copy the UI; the preview is the same component used by guests"

— Engineering Patterns

There are two separate implementations and they share no code. live-experience-panel.component.ts:11 imports GuestShellProjectionComponent — 866 lines re-implementing browse, cart and bill chrome. Guests get guest.page.ts — 2,282 lines. The projection is a props-driven mock with hardcoded fallbacks (venueName = 'Blue Door', greeting = 'Hi there') and it renders sampleItemLabel = 'Classic Burger' — a literal string chosen per experience type, not the venue's catalogue. The document asserts as fact the single property it should be recommending you build. This is the most important sentence in the brief and it is the one that is false.

WRONG
"React/Vue component" · "a JavaScript store (Redux/Vuex) or a React Context"

— Engineering Patterns, Recommendations

The application is Angular 19 standalone components. Neither React, Vue, Redux, Vuex nor React Context appears anywhere in the tree. The equivalent already exists and is already used for the cosmetic fields — Angular signals in StudioContextService. Naming three foreign frameworks in the one section that tells engineers what to build is how a brief gets quietly ignored.

WRONG
"Use WebSockets or a real-time API to push changes to the preview"

— Recommendations, Engineering

The preview is a component in the same browser tab as the editor, roughly 400 pixels to the right of the control that changes it. There is no network hop to optimise. This is a signal write and a re-render. Building it as specified adds a transport, a reconnection path and a whole class of desync bug to solve a problem that does not exist — and you already have a real socket, unauthenticated, that the Operate board never subscribes to.

REWORK
"Debounce input-heavy fields ~500ms · auto-save happens on pauses, updating the central store"

— Engineering Patterns

Right in principle, dangerous against the current store. setup-places.page.ts already debounces at 280ms, and the "central store" is one localStorage key that readWorkspace() JSON-parses on every call and re-serialises with base64 logo and cover data URLs inside it — a 1.2MB logo plus a 1.8MB cover lands at or over the 5MB quota, and QuotaExceededError is uncaught. Follow this advice onto the store as it stands and you reach silent, total save failure faster. Move images to the API before increasing write frequency.

REWORK
"Payments: checkboxes for Card, Apple Pay, Google Pay, Tips, Split Bill"

— Setup Pages UI Patterns

This describes the screen that exists, as though it were the design target. It is a stub. There is no merchant ID, no API key, no bank account, no settlement schedule and no test connection anywhere in Studio — the entire connector API and the PaymentConnectorInstall model are called by nothing from the web app. Worse, the step marks itself complete on arrival. A brief that specifies polish for this screen will produce a beautifully animated toggle set for a venue that still cannot take a cent.

REWORK
The competitor set: Shopify, Webflow, Framer, Notion, Canva, Stripe, Clerk

— Live-Preview Models, comparison table

Seven of the ten rows are website and document builders. They answer "how do live-preview editors work" and not "how does a hospitality ordering product get a venue live". me&u, Mr Yum, Square for Restaurants, Lightspeed and Toast's own onboarding are the comparable set — same buyer, same fifteen-minute setup window, same QR at the end. Only Toast and Square appear, and both only as preview-model rows. The Clerk row is also unresearched: its notes cite Stripe Elements and "ReactLivePreview" instead of Clerk.

REWORK
"The Live Experience panel stays visible at all times (phone frame on desktop)"

— Navigation & IA

The principle is right; the current implementation is not, and the brief does not notice. studio-workspace.component.ts:36 sets max-width: calc(640px + 420px + 6rem) ≈ 1120px, and the preview is always rendered. On an iPad in portrait — the device an operator actually holds — three columns render in roughly 500px of usable width. "At all times" needs a stated breakpoint below which the preview moves under the config rather than beside it.

Take this exactly as written, because it is the fix for a P0. The only hard number in the document, and worth keeping as a gate. Note that the current obstacle is not transport but change detection: no component in the app uses OnPush, and template-bound getters run JSON.parse against localStorage on every tick.

"Never ask a human to imagine"

— Live-Preview Models

The best line in the brief, and the correct organising principle for the whole product — it applies as much to the guest wondering when their food will arrive as to the operator configuring a menu. Keep it. Then notice that the preview currently asks the operator to imagine their menu, because it shows a Classic Burger.

What the brief does not mention, in order of severity
There is no menu editor. Nothing in the web app writes a catalogue item, and the runtime exposes no catalogue write endpoint — the whole surface is read-only and seeded. So "the business owner sees exactly what their guests will see" cannot be true: they see a demo menu with someone else's prices, and the preview beside it shows a hardcoded Classic Burger. Every screen the brief specifies is downstream of a menu that cannot be created. This is the first thing to build, and the document never names it.

Editing while live. The brief's flow ends at Go Live. Changing a price at 8pm with guests mid-order, carts open and food in the kitchen is the actual hard problem in this product, and it is unaddressed — as is whether a live experience can be paused at all, which today it cannot.
The preview has no state. It renders an empty experience: no session, no participants, no cart, no orders in flight. Toggling split-bill, tips, or claim-from-table therefore shows the operator nothing. A preview that cannot demonstrate the features being configured is not a preview; it needs a simulated busy table.
The preview's data source is a live P0. It reads leos.studio.workspace from localStorage — the same key the guest continuity modules wrongly read on a phone that never has it, which is why Pay and Tips toggles have no effect on real guests. Building the preview on that store as recommended entrenches the bug rather than exposing it.
The QR the flow generates is not unique. Go Live produces a QR from defaults.token: 'qr-demo-restaurant', shared by every experience of that type. The publishing diagram's "generate QR code" step has no per-venue token to generate from.
No permissions model. The brief has one undifferentiated "business owner". The product has owners, managers and staff with a real permission vocabulary already written — and no authentication at all in Studio.
Metrics without instrumentation. "Measure setup conversion, time-to-live, error rates, satisfaction" — there is no analytics of any kind in the codebase, and no target numbers are given for any of the four.
Net: adopt the interaction model, the language discipline and the motion spec. Discard the engineering section and rewrite it against Angular signals and a single shared guest component. Treat "the preview is literally the guest component" not as a stated fact but as the first engineering task — it is the one change that makes every other recommendation in the brief true.

Credit where due

What's genuinely good
Do not lose these in the rebuild
The continuity modules. operate-glance.ts, grow-breath.ts, team-confidence.ts and the *-continuity.ts family are pure, dependency-free, injectable-clock, unit-tested functions with no DOM coupling. This is the healthiest layer in the codebase and the bugs are in where they're wired, not what they do.
The outbox write path is correct. OutboxService.append takes an optional transaction client and every important write passes it, so events and state commit atomically. Only the publisher half is broken.
Claim-from-table with a real undo. The server returns previousParticipantId per line and the client replays it — one tap to claim, one tap to undo, no allocation wizard. This is the best-designed interaction in the product.
PayFast signature encoding is right. Uppercase hex, + for spaces, the !'()* escapes, insertion order preserved rather than sorted, empty values skipped. This is the detail most PayFast integrations get wrong.
Permission language that speaks human. "They can see / act / finish" with a why sentence per group, and sessionRevokeCopy answering "is it safe to end this login?" with consequence-then-aftermath. That is real product thinking about an operator's actual anxiety.
The staff-facing API shows you already know how to do this. FulfilmentController.updateStatus chains a guard, a permission decorator, an explicit org check and a station-access check. The pattern the Studio endpoints are missing is already written, in your own codebase, twenty lines away.
prefers-reduced-motion honoured consistently across Studio, and safeGuestImageUrl allow-listing image schemes rather than blocklisting. Both are the kind of thing teams skip.
One thing worth saying plainly: the reason this teardown is so long is that there is enough here to be worth tearing down. A thin product produces a short list.
